How to interpret this experimental guidance

Key takeaway: Test-set analysis indicates that the 60-km configuration provides the most balanced guidance, followed closely by the 75-km configuration.

This is an experimental forecast product. Across the test set, the ML guidance showed better average skill than the WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook for Moderate-or-greater risks. These results describe average test-set performance and do not guarantee better performance for every event.

What the ML predicts

Each configuration predicts the probability that observed rainfall will exceed Flash Flood Guidance somewhere within a specified radius (r). Flash Flood Guidance estimates the amount of rainfall needed to produce flash flooding. The four configurations use radii of 40, 60, 75, and 100 km.

  • r = 60 km: Most balanced overall in the test-set analysis.
  • r = 75 km: A close second, with somewhat broader and stronger risk areas.
  • r = 40 km: Often too conservative in both risk-area size and severity.
  • r = 100 km: Often too aggressive; risk areas can be too large, with high probabilities issued too frequently.

What “Practically Perfect” means

Practically Perfect is an observation-based verification benchmark created after the valid period from observed flood-proxy locations. The observations are spatially expanded and smoothed to show an idealized placement of risk areas. It is not a forecast and is displayed only for verification.
